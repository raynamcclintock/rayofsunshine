---
title: Is it immoral to stop feeling the weight of every bad thing I know about at every moment in time
date: 2026-10-02
---

Probably certainly not...

But knowingly doing harm (living a daily life in America paying taxes, using my iphone, driving and flying somewhere just for fun, buying food from companies that are poisoning us) should be immoral. Because we know the negative consequences.

Where is the line - that you should dwell on what you can truly control. But I am in control of my actions and how I spend my time and money. Should I try to live off grid in the woods and grow all my own food. Or should I fully participate and try to get in the big business rooms where the systematic decisions are actually being made. Is the moral path the most effective? And if not does the morality shift to what is effective when thinking about climate change? (taking a utilitarian approach). I know the answer lies in between in the nuance but where is the line?

Does worrying about it all do anything? I am mostly vegetarian for climate reasons and studying carbon sequestration techniques in a PhD program which is more nothing, but my attempt at this has been ineffective and I am sitting here with a microbial thesis that no one implementing mCDR will probably care about. It is not enough but the worry and frustration doesn't make it any more effective. Maybe it could if I am sharing these feeling and urgency and frustration and momentum and despair with others, moving the collective conscious toward the hope tinged terror that I am always feeling. 

I am so privileged to be this existential. And at the same time I am a poor graduate student in a shoebox studio who is hoping the bar she's meeting friends at later still takes cash in 2026 because I grocery shopped today and now there is less than $50 in my bank account.
